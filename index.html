Bringing up a GPU rack is still run on spreadsheets.

New hardware has to be burned in before it carries real work. Today the evidence for that lives in a dozen places, and none of them agree.

Where is it?

Rack, U position and cabling live in spreadsheets and DCIM tools that drift from the floor.

What failed?

Test logs name a hostname and a GPU index, not the tray, slot and serial a technician has to pull.

Can we prove it?

Acceptance, RMA cases and handovers rely on screenshots and PDFs that nobody can check later.

How it works

One definition of your site, one run command, one record of what happened.

Define

Describe the floor, racks and cabling in plain YAML, or import them from NetBox or a spreadsheet. Shapes such as DGX B200 or GB200 NVL72 come from a shared catalog.

Run

Pick a burn-in profile and run it on a host, a rack or a row. Rodage drives the tools you already trust: DCGM, NCCL tests, nvme-cli and Redfish.

See

Results land on the rack as you watch. Each tray, GPU, drive and power supply turns green, amber or red, with the error and its history one click away.

Prove

Every run becomes an append-only record with serials, firmware and log hashes: an acceptance report, an RMA packet or a supplier handover.

Readable by people. Runnable by machines.

A burn-in profile says what to test and what passing means. It is versioned, so every result points at the exact rules it was judged by, and two sites running the same profile get comparable answers.

Pass thresholds can be absolute or relative to your fleet, so a GPU at 90% of its neighbors' bandwidth is flagged before it slows a training job.

# profiles/gb200-burnin.yaml
kind: BurnInProfile
metadata: { name: gb200-burnin, version: 3 }
spec:
  shape: nvidia-gb200-nvl72
  stages:
    - name: gpu-diagnostics
      tool: dcgm-diag
      pass: { level: 3, xidErrors: 0 }
    - name: nccl-allreduce
      tool: nccl-tests
      warn: { busBw: "< 95% of fleet median" }
      fail: { busBw: "< 85% of fleet median" }
    - name: power-redundancy
      tool: redfish-psu-check

Built for the people on the floor

Rodage shows where hardware is, what it is made of and what happened to it. It does not try to replace your monitoring.

Every part, in place

Racks are drawn as their real shape, and trays as their field-replaceable units, so a failure points at a slot and a serial.

History that follows the part

Tests, reseats, swaps and RMAs stay attached to both the slot and the serial, even after the part moves.

Swap it, or open a case

Each part is marked customer-replaceable or vendor-serviced, with a link to the service manual.

Evidence you can check

Records are append-only, with log hashes and optional signatures, so a result can be trusted months later.

Works with what you have

Import from NetBox or CSV, link tickets in Jira or ServiceNow, and export topology for Slurm and NCCL.

Open and vendor-neutral

NVIDIA, AMD and OEM servers in one view. Planned under Apache 2.0, with an open spec anyone can implement.

Who it's for

GPU clouds

Fast-growing fleets with small Ops teams and no time for spreadsheets.

Colocation providers

Hosting GPU customers who want proof their racks are healthy.

HPC and research centers

University and lab clusters that prefer open tools they can inspect.

System integrators

Ship racks with burn-in evidence the customer can re-run and compare.

Early and in the open

Rodage is in early development. A working prototype covers the site definition, the rack and FRU console, and a validator. Test execution is next. If you run GPU hardware and want to shape what gets built, get in touch.
